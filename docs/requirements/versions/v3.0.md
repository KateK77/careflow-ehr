# CareFlow EHR v3.0 — Complete Product Requirements

Document revision: 1.0. Consolidated November 16, 2026.
Product scope: the blue-interface EHR, username-based patient/staff registration and separately configured synthetic public hosting.
This is the final coherent v3 requirement set. It is not a concatenation of prompts and feedback.

## 1. Product purpose and version boundary

CareFlow EHR v3.0 is an independently developed, server-rendered Django EHR and hospital-workflow demonstration that staff and patients can enter through a polished shared website. It combines a complete synthetic clinical/administrative platform with visible registration, clear staff approval rules and a professional blue visual identity.

The product supports registration, appointments, encounters, signed SOAP, history, nursing, laboratory, medication/pharmacy/MAR, patient communication, billing, imaging, inpatient and ED operations. All requirements are specified in this document without relying on previous-version descriptions.

The final account design is **Username + Password + Confirm password, with no email requirement**. No mailbox verification, email code, sending service or email-based recovery belongs to this version. "Revision 3.1" in older requirement filenames records this final scope correction; it is not a fourth website generation or a claim of a separately released v3.1 product.

Implementation is tracked by PR #61, `feature/v3-account-registration`, head `f7e171e0f25606b3635aae36fde1102493d68789`. Recorded public deployment uses application snapshot `a9ae1885ee9a98fad6687697db1fcb2581540c75`. A hosted branch snapshot does not mean the application stack has merged into GitHub main.

## 2. Public website, registration and staff approval

### 2.1 Visual identity and navigation

**V3-01.** Use blue as the primary brand/action color with professional typography, restrained neutral surfaces and appropriate success/warning/error colors. Do not make the entire interface green. Maintain original CareFlow styling and a visible DEMO — NOT FOR CLINICAL USE notice.

**V3-02.** The landing page prominently exposes Staff Sign In, Patient Sign In, Create Patient Account and Apply for Staff Access. Each corresponding login page also exposes its registration path. Both portals must use real authentication; staff and patient sessions remain correctly isolated.

**V3-03.** Use a responsive staff shell with grouped authorized modules, current user/role and location. Provide an independently scoped patient shell. Present real dashboard counts, an authorized clinic-local agenda and a working patient appointment-request entry. No placeholder feature buttons or fabricated patient data.

**V3-04.** Retain compact patient/appointment tables, readable MRNs and status labels, grouped demographics and clear View Patient actions. Preserve mobile navigation and scrollable tables without page-wide overflow. Login text must not claim there are only four staff roles.

### 2.2 Patient self-registration

**V3-05.** Require a unique username, password, matching confirmation and an explicit unchecked synthetic-data consent control. Use the current username contract: 3–40 lowercase letters, digits, dots, hyphens or underscores. Reject case-insensitive duplicate usernames. Apply Django password validation, including at least 12 characters and rejection of common or all-numeric passwords.

**V3-06.** Create the account, enrollment/audit history and a NEW fictional patient chart transactionally. Use a new synthetic MRN and documented fictional placeholders. Never attach a registration to an existing chart based on submitted MRN, name, DOB, email, URL parameter or guessed identifier.

**V3-07.** A valid new patient may sign in immediately through the Patient Portal and see only the chart bound to that account. Missing consent, mismatched/invalid passwords and duplicate usernames must not leave partial accounts or charts. Render helpful field errors without echoing passwords.

### 2.3 Staff applications and approval

**V3-08.** Staff apply with username/password/confirmation, explicit synthetic consent and one permitted requested role: Physician, Nurse, FrontDesk, Lab Staff, Pharmacist, BillingStaff, RadiologyStaff or HospitalOperations. Public applicants cannot choose Administrator or obtain superuser/server privileges.

**V3-09.** Persist new staff as inactive, pending review and without an effective clinical role. Registration success must clearly state that an Administrator must approve the application before staff login. Submission is not approval, and a matching username is not authorization.

**V3-10.** A verified Administrator reviews the current application version, explicitly approves or rejects with a reason and chooses a permitted granted role. Prevent stale/conflicting reviews, self-approval and activation of accounts with incompatible portal/role state. An approval activates only the intended account/role and records reviewer, time, reason and outcome. A rejection preserves the record and denies login.

**V3-11.** A pending applicant who enters the correct password receives clear approval-pending guidance while remaining unauthenticated. Wrong passwords, nonexistent users, rejected applications and unrelated inactive accounts retain generic failures. Patient login must not disclose staff application status. Do not weaken authentication to fix confusing messaging.

**V3-12.** Preserve existing accounts and passwords during UI or documentation changes. Username/password signup does not establish professional identity or employment. There is no self-service email recovery or implemented public password-change interface to advertise.

### 2.4 Forms and feedback behavior

**V3-13.** Clinical patient registration is distinct from user-account registration. Group patient identity, optional contacts and explicit synthetic confirmation; provide keyboard-operable Sex radios including Unknown. Only show duplicate-review confirmation when backend duplicate detection requires it. Never auto-check required confirmation.

**V3-14.** Appointment search remains optional and visibly separate from required Patient, Physician, Appointment start and Review appointment controls. Preserve review/confirmation, correct patient/physician linkage, Scheduled state, check-in and audit persistence.

**V3-15.** Show readable validation and audit labels, retained invalid-form input, correctly linked resolved problems and structured report previews. Avoid duplicated allergy errors, raw internal field/constraint identifiers, mojibake and obsolete availability text. Use correct singular/plural units and pre-submission fictional dose/reference guidance. Never rewrite historical clinical content to improve its display.

### 2.5 Account architecture and abuse controls

**V3-16.** Add an accounts module with Enrollment, AccountEvent and bounded RequestBucket state alongside Django User/Group and patient bindings. Signup and approval are transactional; review uses version checks; account events preserve historical decisions.

**V3-17.** Keep CSRF protection, session authentication, secure password hashes and bounded login/signup requests by identity and trusted client-address source. Do not trust arbitrary forwarded headers. Handle rate-limit/database contention with explicit safe responses. Shared proxy addresses can aggregate visitors; this remains an operational limit to validate, not permission to remove throttling.

### 2.6 Local and hosted operation

**V3-18.** The local preview uses a new synthetic database per launch and does not resume, migrate or reseed previous previews. A developer supplies the local demo password privately; no password is hardcoded in requirements or Git. Seed basic fictional patient/account/appointment data without claiming every advanced workspace is populated.

**V3-19.** Hosted configuration is separate from local settings: DEBUG false, explicit HTTPS origin/hosts, a new strong server secret, secure cookies/CSRF, locally served collected static assets and an explicit persistent SQLite path outside source. No email service is required. A GitHub source URL alone is not a Django deployment.

**V3-20.** The recorded live site is [jiawen.pythonanywhere.com](https://jiawen.pythonanywhere.com/), with [Staff Sign In](https://jiawen.pythonanywhere.com/login/) and [Patient Sign In](https://jiawen.pythonanywhere.com/portal/login/). Initial deployment provisioned a NEW synthetic hosted database rather than copying local patient databases. Free hosting requires capacity/renewal management and separately approved operational changes; no charges are authorized by this document.

**V3-21.** Existing account login is not evidence that public shared access is isolated or resettable. Visitor sandboxes, automatic resets, passwordless public role switching and public credential publication are not implemented v3 requirements. They remain separate decisions. Do not relabel existing writable/private credentials as restricted public accounts through a documentation edit.

## 3. Roles and authorization

| Role | Permitted product responsibilities | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Administrator | Patient administration, operational setup and the administrative actions explicitly supported by each module | Not an automatic substitute for a treating physician, pharmacist, billing specialist or server operator |
| Physician | Own schedule, authorized patient charts and encounters, SOAP, history, diagnoses, orders and result review | No demographic editing through the physician role alone; no access to another physician's restricted authoring context |
| Nurse | Authorized nursing worklist, rooming, vitals, specimen collection, MAR and clinical read views | Cannot author or sign physician SOAP; nursing writes require eligible appointment/encounter state |
| FrontDesk | Patient registration and demographic edits, appointment management and check-in | No detailed clinical-note authoring or unrestricted clinical-chart access |
| Lab Staff | Laboratory queue, assigned specimens, report entry, publication and authorized corrections/withdrawals | Assignment and patient/order context limit report access; no unrelated SOAP or demographic administration |
| Pharmacist | Signed medication queue, assignment, clarification, verification and simulated dispensing | Unclaimed queue exposes minimum necessary information; no unrelated full-chart access |
| BillingStaff | Synthetic coverage, charge catalog, statements, claims and payment ledger | No general inpatient, ED or clinical-note access |
| RadiologyStaff | Assigned imaging workflow, examination and report management | No unrelated clinical records or arbitrary physician authoring |
| HospitalOperations | Facilities, admission, bed allocation, transfer, discharge and supported ED operations | Clinical read and physician/nurse authoring remain distinct |
| Patient | Own linked appointments, released records, messages, appointment requests and released billing information | No staff portal access, other patient records or unreleased results |

**C-01.** Validate permissions in Django views and workflow services for both GET and POST. Hiding navigation does not grant or enforce access. Reject forged object IDs and wrong-patient/wrong-encounter combinations.

**C-02.** Preserve the existing role model: some staff demographic/nursing read scopes are broad, while physician encounter authoring and specialist assignments are narrower. Do not claim per-visitor tenant isolation or universally least-privilege chart access that the prototype does not implement.

**C-03.** Patient accounts bind to one fictional patient through a protected relationship. A user with incompatible staff membership must not acquire patient access merely by opening a portal URL. A username such as "administrator" does not itself grant a role.

## 4. Patient registration and scheduling

**C-04. Patient record.** Persist MRN, first/last name, date of birth, sex, optional gender identity, contact details, address and emergency contact. Derive age from DOB. Validate required values, date bounds and allowed input. Enforce MRN uniqueness including case-insensitive collisions at the database boundary.

**C-05. Directory.** Provide a real database-backed patient table, filters/search by supported identity fields, details and authorized demographic editing. Display clear no-results and empty states. Keep current patient identity and MRN visible when navigating.

**C-06. Duplicate review.** Warn about potential identity duplicates without equating every similar name with an existing patient. Require explicit duplicate-review confirmation when the backend detects a potential duplicate. A successful creation or edit produces an audit event and persists after refresh.

**C-07. Scheduling.** Choose an existing patient and authorized physician, start time, duration, appointment type and visit reason. Separate optional patient search from booking data. Review the appointment before confirmation. Reject conflicting physician appointments and invalid dates/durations; a rejected conflict must not appear in the calendar.

**C-08. Appointment lifecycle.** Support scheduled appointments, rescheduling, cancellation with reason and check-in to Arrived, with actor, timestamp and transition history. A cancelled slot can be booked again if otherwise eligible. Replayed or stale commands must not duplicate an appointment or overwrite a later edit.

**C-09. Check-in.** Verify patient/appointment linkage and current version before arrival. Display the responsible staff member and arrival time. Keep appointment state separate from encounter state; completing an encounter does not silently rewrite appointment history.

## 5. Physician workspace, chart and clinical history

**C-10. Physician workspace.** Show the clinician's authorized appointments and recent encounters, current statuses and actions that open the correct patient/encounter. A physician can start an encounter only from an eligible arrived appointment and must not start a duplicate encounter.

**C-11. Chart.** Organize overview, encounters, clinical notes, medical history, problems, allergies, medication history, medication orders, nursing flowsheet, laboratory and encounter diagnoses around one persistent patient context. Show accessible recent information with source timestamps. Missing measurements must not be fabricated.

**C-12. SOAP.** Save Subjective, Objective, Assessment and Plan as a versioned draft; validate author, encounter state and explicit synthetic confirmation. Signing records signer, time and version and makes the signed content read-only. Encounter completion requires the supported signed-documentation prerequisites. Subsequent lab/pharmacy activity must leave SOAP content, version and signing time unchanged.

**C-13. History.** Maintain past medical, surgical, family and social history with dates, comments, source/provenance, author and review status. Provide create/view/edit/review and audit events through authorized clinical actions.

**C-14. Problems and diagnoses.** Preserve active, inactive and resolved longitudinal problems, onset/resolution dates and optional mock terminology. Encounter diagnoses have primary/secondary classification, correct patient/encounter association and optional problem linkage. Reject a second primary diagnosis with a readable error. Show the linked problem's actual state, including Resolved. Only the authorized physician authors diagnoses in an in-progress encounter; completed diagnoses cannot be modified.

**C-15. Allergies.** Record allergen, reaction, severity, clinical/verification state, reviewer and times. Distinguish unknown, not reviewed, no known allergies and documented allergies. Reject contradictory assessments; preserve submitted values on validation errors. Do not render internal field keys or constraint names as patient-facing messages.

**C-16. Medication history.** Maintain current, stopped and historical statements with fictional name, dose description, route, frequency, dates and patient-reported/documented provenance. Review history is distinct from medication ordering, dispensing and administration.

## 6. Nursing and medication administration

**C-17. Rooming and vitals.** Provide an authorized nursing worklist, chief complaint, nursing observations, pain score, provider-readiness marker and vital panels. Supported measurements include blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, temperature with units, oxygen saturation, height and weight with units. Record observation time, recorder, patient and appointment; validate values, units and eligible state.

**C-18. Flowsheet.** Persist and display longitudinal nursing observations with version history and source context. Physician chart summaries may show the latest authorized assessment and latest vital panel from different appointments; identify both sources rather than implying they are one observation.

**C-19. MAR.** Support only simulated administration associated with eligible signed medication/dispensing context. Record nurse, patient verification, observation time, fictional quantity and outcome. Support documented omission/refusal and append-only correction/void history as implemented; never present these as real medication administration.

**C-20. Preservation.** Reject cross-patient, stale, unauthorized or invalid-state nursing/MAR writes. A correction must preserve the original event and must not edit a signed physician note. No clinical dosing or treatment recommendation is generated.

## 7. Laboratory orders and results

**C-21. Catalog and ordering.** Use revisioned fictional panels and typed component definitions. Authorized physicians create/edit drafts, review and submit orders in the correct encounter. Repeated intentional orders need explicit confirmation where required. Retain catalog snapshots and reject stale revisions or duplicate commands without lost updates.

**C-22. Specimens.** Record collection with patient identity confirmation, collector, times and specimen/accession context. Maintain one active attempt and retain rejected-attempt history. Receipt, rejection, recollection and cancellation must obey the supported lifecycle rather than silently reuse invalid specimens.

**C-23. Processing and publication.** Lab Staff claim authorized work, receive specimens, enter validated typed values/units, preview a structured report and explicitly finalize it. The same assigned staff member may enter and finalize in this synthetic MVP; this is not independent production verification. Record entry/finalizer identities, timestamps and versions.

**C-24. Separate states.** Treat order, specimen, report publication and physician review as independent states. A saved current draft is not a historical published revision. Completing the encounter must not prevent legitimate later specimen/result processing.

**C-25. Corrections and withdrawal.** Publish a new revision when correcting a finalized report. Never overwrite or delete the earlier publication. Preserve reasons, authors, previous acknowledgements and version-specific history. Withdrawal/entered-in-error must remain visibly distinct from a negative or absent result. Context-error quarantine restricts affected report presentation.

**C-26. Review and reassignment.** Acknowledge the exact current report revision with the responsible physician identity. A corrected report requires renewed review. If an ordering physician becomes inactive, only explicitly authorized reassignment with a reason may establish a covering physician; preserve the original order author and previous reviews without granting unrelated chart or SOAP permissions.

## 8. Medication orders and pharmacy

**C-27. Medication catalog and draft.** Use fictional catalog codes and revisions, name, dosage form, strength, route and frequency. Compose dose description, duration, quantity and instructions. Explain the fictional dose format before submission. Do not accept these records as prescriptions for actual medicines.

**C-28. Signature and cancellation.** Validate encounter, physician, version, catalog snapshot and explicit confirmation before signing. Retain draft and signing history. Cancellation follows the supported pre-processing boundary and preserves the original signature and reason; cancelled orders are not available as unclaimed work.

**C-29. Pharmacy handoff.** Expose minimal information in the unclaimed signed-order queue. Claiming binds review to a pharmacist. Support clarification requests and physician responses without modifying signed order content, approval/rejection with reason and an eligible simulated dispense.

**C-30. Dispensing.** Persist fictional quantity, actor and event history. Deny dispensing for rejected, cancelled or otherwise ineligible orders, including direct URLs and POSTs. Prevent duplicate processing through version/replay protection. Permit legitimate verification and dispensing after encounter completion without reopening or rewriting SOAP.

## 9. Patient Portal

**C-31. Patient experience.** Show the authenticated patient's dashboard, appointments, accessible visit/medication information, released results, messages, requests and released billing records. New or empty datasets receive honest empty states, not fabricated clinical information.

**C-32. Result visibility.** Only explicitly released, eligible laboratory revisions are patient-visible. Report correction, withdrawal and context errors must affect eligibility consistently. Direct object URLs must not expose another patient's result or an unreleased result.

**C-33. Messaging.** Patients exchange synthetic messages with authorized physicians; show conversation history and unread/read state. Validate thread ownership, actor and version. Sharing an account also shares that patient's conversations; no private visitor separation is implied.

**C-34. Appointment requests.** Patients request new appointments, rescheduling and cancellation. Requests remain distinct from confirmed appointments until authorized staff review. Approval must perform the normal scheduling validation and audit linkage.

## 10. Billing and simulated insurance

**C-35. Coverage and statements.** Store fictional coverage revisions, effective dates and status; use revisioned charge catalogs and itemized encounter charges. Finalize a statement into historical line snapshots and explicitly release it for patient viewing. Closed financial history must not be silently edited.

**C-36. Claims.** Support simulated submission, denial, appeal and supported approval/partial-approval responses with reasons and history. No real payer connection, insurance verification, money transfer or claim submission occurs.

**C-37. Ledger.** Record simulated patient/payer payments and append-only reversals. References must use the SYN- prefix, be globally unique and follow the allowed uppercase-letter/digit/hyphen format. Each reversal uses a new reference. Validate amounts and remaining balances without floating-point monetary ambiguity.

**C-38. Isolation.** BillingStaff permissions do not confer detailed clinical, ED or inpatient access. Patients see only their own released statements and payment history. Reject forged financial object references and duplicate transactions.

## 11. Imaging and radiology

**C-39. Order and scheduling.** Use a fictional revisioned imaging catalog, physician draft/signature, radiology assignment, scheduled examination and examination workflow. Keep order, exam, report and review state separate.

**C-40. Catalog conflicts.** On stale catalog edits, identify the fictional code, explain the stale revision, show the latest revision when authorized and offer reload/reapply guidance. Do not retry automatically, duplicate revisions or overwrite newer data; preserve identical-command replay protection.

**C-41. Reporting.** Provide preliminary/final reports with provenance, corrections as new history and physician acknowledgement of the current revision. Corrected reports require re-acknowledgement. Context-error withdrawal preserves history and restricts visibility.

**C-42. Simulation boundary.** The authorized examination workflow currently permits starting before the scheduled time. Document that behavior as a local simulation rule, not a production scheduling guarantee or an implicit request to introduce a timing restriction. No PACS/DICOM integration or actual image diagnosis is included.

## 12. Inpatient and emergency operations

**C-43. Facilities and admission.** Model department, ward, room and bed; support admission requests, attending assignment and an active inpatient stay. Allocate beds transactionally and reject competing active allocations.

**C-44. Transfer and discharge.** Transfer to a valid available bed, preserve movement history, record discharge planning and summary, then discharge and release the bed. Enforce patient/stay context and version checks; do not replace the clinical encounter or signed SOAP records.

**C-45. Emergency workflow.** Support registration/waiting, fictional triage, rooming, provider review, observation and disposition. Maintain actor/timestamp history and reject invalid transitions or conflicting room occupancy.

**C-46. ED-to-inpatient.** Admission from ED must coordinate the inpatient stay/bed with the ED disposition and release the ED room. Discharge also releases the room. Billing or other unrelated roles cannot gain access through direct hospital URLs.

## 13. Persistent data, architecture and audit requirements

Use Python and Django 5.2 with server-rendered templates, local CSS/JavaScript and SQLite for the demonstration. Keep the modular monolith: core, patients, scheduling, clinical, history, nursing, labs, medications, mar, portal, billing, imaging and hospital. Do not replace this with React/FastAPI or interpret the original greenfield technology proposal as an instruction to rebuild.

| Domain | Principal persistent records and relationships |
| --- | --- |
| Identity/patients | Django User/Group, Patient, AuditEvent; protected patient ownership for portal users |
| Scheduling/clinical | Appointment and events → patient/physician; Encounter → appointment/patient/physician; versioned Note and ClinicalEvent |
| History/nursing | Medical history, problems, allergies, statements, diagnoses, assessments/vitals and review/event records |
| Laboratory | Revisioned catalog → order → specimen attempts → report revisions; review assignment/acknowledgement and LabEvent |
| Medication/MAR | Catalog snapshot → draft/signed order → pharmacist review/dispense → simulated administration/exception history |
| Portal | Patient binding, result release, message thread/messages and appointment requests |
| Billing | Coverage/catalog revisions, statement/charges/snapshots, claim events, payments and reversals |
| Imaging | Catalog/order, examination workflow, report revisions and physician reviews |
| Hospital | Department/ward/room/bed, admission request/stay, ED visit and hospital events |

**C-47. Persistence.** Refreshing a page or restarting the application must not lose committed data. Relationship validation must reject cross-patient joins even when object IDs individually exist.

**C-48. Transactions.** Apply the existing transaction boundaries, provider/operation serialization, uniqueness/check constraints, optimistic versions and command-idempotency receipts. A failed command must not leave an unrelated audit, allocation or partial clinical write. SQLite concurrency limitations remain explicit; unrestricted production concurrency is not assumed.

**C-49. Audit.** Record actor, time, action, relevant patient/encounter, reason and changed fields/state as appropriate. Display readable action labels while preserving raw historical payloads. Do not rewrite a long historical comment to improve its appearance. Application append-only rules are not proof of database-level tamper resistance against privileged SQL.

**C-50. Time and storage.** Store timezone-aware timestamps and display the configured local timezone. Validate observation and workflow chronology without inventing missing values. Use additive migrations and isolated upgrade rehearsals; never reset, overwrite, reseed or migrate an existing accepted database as a testing shortcut.

## 14. Security, operation and engineering quality

**C-51. Access.** Retain Django session authentication, password hashing, CSRF, input validation, escaped output and server-side scope checks. Denied pages must not reveal protected values. Do not treat role names or patient IDs from a browser as authority.

**C-52. Synthetic boundary.** Every product screen must display DEMO — NOT FOR CLINICAL USE. Users must explicitly confirm synthetic data where required. This confirmation is not an automated guarantee that arbitrary free text contains no personal information.

**C-53. Secrets and preservation.** Keep database files, private passwords, API keys, deployment secrets and sensitive logs out of source control and screenshots. Preserve existing local/preview databases, signed documentation, report revisions, audit trails, Chinese requirements, English translations and original approved prompts.

**C-54. Quality workflow.** Use Issue → Branch → Commit → PR → Review → Merge. Record requirements, file changes, real test commands/results and known gaps in English. Do not force-push, merge, deploy or create a release without the required separate approval.

**C-55. Verification.** Run Django checks, migration consistency and the relevant full regression suite on new synthetic test databases. Browser tests should exercise actual navigation, form submission, state persistence and role denial. Inspect responsive layouts, required controls, static CSS and direct object requests.

**C-56. Evidence labels.** Separate user-reported manual observations, scripted Chromium results, offline CUA rehearsals and live native OpenAI Computer Use evaluation. Passing one category does not establish another. Paid API use needs its own authorization; no API call is required to use the EHR itself.

## 16. Explicit exclusions and limitations

This version is an educational synthetic-data prototype, not a certified EHR, clinical decision-support system, prescribing/administration product, payment processor or production healthcare deployment. It does not claim HIPAA/ONC compliance, exhaustive security, full accessibility, production concurrency or independent clinical verification.

Do not add external labs/pharmacies/payers, real patient data, diagnostic/treatment recommendations, clinical AI/LLM features, FHIR interfaces, PACS, broad analytics, smart phrases, autosave, signed-note addenda or complete AVS workflows merely because they appeared in the original long-term vision. These need separately approved implementation where not already delivered.

Configurable appointment-type catalogs, physician working hours/leave, revised early check-in policy and broader human-readable historical identifier resolution remain deferred. Do not silently turn deferred UX suggestions into current defects or approved business rules. No per-visitor public sandbox, automatic shared-data reset or complete populated tour for every role is implied.

## 17. Version-specific acceptance

**V3-A01.** A recruiter/user can identify the blue CareFlow identity, two real portal sign-ins and both registration paths at desktop/mobile widths. Static styles load and the demo notice remains visible.

**V3-A02.** Patient signup accepts a unique valid username, valid matching passwords and explicit synthetic consent; it creates one new fictional chart and permits patient login. Duplicate username, invalid password or missing consent leaves no partial records.

**V3-A03.** A staff application persists as inactive/pending without an effective role. Correct pending credentials show approval guidance without a session; wrong/unknown/rejected credentials do not disclose pending state.

**V3-A04.** Only an Administrator reviews a current application with a reason. Approval activates the intended supported role; stale/conflicting/self-review or forged role requests fail. The approved staff member can sign in using their own existing registration password.

**V3-A05.** Patient/staff boundaries, direct GET/POST authorization, CSRF, patient ownership and clinical business rules remain effective through signup, approval and login.

**V3-A06.** No registration email field, verification code, delivery requirement or email recovery is needed. Optional demographic email is a patient contact field, not an authentication or ownership requirement.

**V3-A07.** Registration keyboard controls, duplicate confirmation, booking review, readable validation/audits and responsive clinical tables work as described; no historical content is edited to satisfy UI checks.

**V3-A08.** Hosted startup fails closed for missing/insecure configuration. Verify HTTPS, cookies, CSRF, static assets, persistent storage and a fresh-only provisioning path before advertising a deployment. Do not label unverified public accounts available.

**V3-A09.** Run the complete connected acceptance journeys A-01–A-14 on isolated synthetic fixtures and compare protected records before/after. Record actual test coverage and remaining limits.

## 18. Verification record, operation and limits

Historical final staff-login verification records **536 Django tests and 32 local Chromium workflows**. The later public deployment records **nine scripted Chromium checks** of actual HTTPS landing/CSS/mobile presentation, patient login/signup, pending staff/approval/role denial and secure cookies. These are historical reported results, not new tests run for this description or exhaustive public-host clinical/security acceptance.

The separate CUA-100 evaluation records **85/100** task success, **336 API responses** and **$3.38 estimated token cost, not an invoice**. It requested GPT-5.4 through the OpenAI Responses API/native ComputerToolset, with framework-assisted setup/authentication and Playwright execution. It does not prove complete autonomous login/navigation, full provider-side provenance, clinical readiness or success of every workflow. [Full audit](../../showcase/portfolio/cua-evidence-audit.md) and [original results](../../testing/cua100-v1/results.md) preserve failures and provenance limitations.

From the v3 application source, use `python tools/v3_preview.py --port 8015` with the project's installed dependencies and a privately entered new demo password. Each launch creates a fresh preview; do not use it to resume a valuable existing database. Advanced clinical workflows are supported but basic preview seeds do not populate every queue.

Public hosting is an authorized branch deployment, not evidence that the v3 application is merged into main. Do not clone the current main and assume it contains every v3 registration feature. Host capacity, renewal, backup operations, shared-proxy rate limiting and comprehensive accessibility/concurrency/security remain limitations. Public account/data-isolation work needs separate approval.
